## Core business thesis
DAMB GOOD LAH is not primarily a conventional restaurant and should not be designed around one high-commission marketplace order at a time.

Primary economic lanes:
1. Office/corporate drop-offs
2. Catering and preorders
3. Direct pickup/direct ordering
4. Repeat direct customers
5. Low-commission marketplace discovery/overflow

Keep the food ambitious and the business conservative. Prove demand first; add fixed cost second. Every expense must materially improve food, speed, unit cost, compliance or reliable sales.

## HALAL — hard constraint
The commercial concept is being designed as **fully HALAL**.
- No pork in commercial inventory or production.
- Do not make a public certified-HALAL claim until certifier approval.
- Use Malaysian Muslim culinary precedent first instead of reinventing established halal adaptations.
- CKT keeps authentic Chinese-sausage character through **certified halal chicken lap cheong / la chang**.
- Generic chicken sausage/frankfurter is not acceptable.
- Review all processed SKUs at item level: belacan, fish sauce, soy sauces, kecap manis, ketchup/flavor systems if relevant, frying oil, ghee, wrappers, blends, processing aids.
- Kitchen arrangement must be preflighted with chosen certifier before long-term commitment.

Current certification candidates: HFSAA first, IFANCA as comparison.

## Protected culinary architecture
Keep four distinct flavor lanes:
1. Padang curry
2. House sambal with dish-specific finishing
3. Dedicated Mamak-style mee goreng sauce
4. Protected CKT seasoning/chile/high-heat lane

Shared prep where useful:
- one main chicken prep
- one yellow noodle SKU where appropriate
- separate fresh flat rice noodle for CKT
- shared potato prep
- one firm tofu SKU, tender vs crisp treatments
- advance-prep dry hitters where safe to preserve service-time fryer capacity

CKT is a benchmark dish. Do not simplify it into generic noodles to accommodate weak equipment. If the kitchen cannot produce proper CKT, either solve the equipment problem or do not launch CKT from that facility.

## Economics — important conclusions
Food cost is not the main problem. **Throughput + channel cost + labor structure + kitchen cost + packaging** dominate.

Planning targets:
- Consumer economic productivity: **>=18 main-equivalents per total paid kitchen hour**
- Corporate constrained-menu productivity: **>=24 per total paid kitchen hour**
- Corporate drop preference: **20+ meals per destination**
- Food waste: ~5% or better after stabilization
- Consumer packaging: ~ $1.15/main or better if quality permits
- Corporate packaging: ~ $0.65/meal
- Avoid 25-30% marketplace commissions as the business foundation

At current v0.2 planning assumptions and ~18 economic mains/hour, approximate fully loaded production costs before payment/channel leakage:
- Chicken Rice ~ $9.0
- Mee Goreng ~ $9.1
- Nasi Lemak ~ $9.6
- Curry Mee ~ $9.7
- Beef Rice ~ $10.2
- CKT ~ $11.4

These are estimates, not measured costs.

Key lever: moving from 10 to 18-20 main-equivalents per paid kitchen hour cuts several dollars per meal in labor+kitchen allocation. Do not chase 20-cent ingredient savings while ignoring multi-dollar throughput losses.

## Corporate/catering strategy
Corporate is not automatically a discount channel. It should become a **margin engine** because one order can replace 10-50 individual transactions and reduce delivery, acquisition, waste and batching cost.

Potential corporate pricing should likely sit above the consumer value menu when service/labeling/delivery/convenience justify it; do not assume $11.95 corporate pricing is optimal.

Large office orders can be encouraged toward 2-3 main choices for real batching efficiency.

Acquire initial recurring offices manually before spending heavily on paid ads. Strong prospect roles include office managers, executive assistants, People/HR teams, lab/clinic managers and department coordinators.

Use social automation for legitimate content creation, scheduling, CRM, reporting and testing. Do not use fake engagement bots.

## Employee strategy

Measure:
- paid person-hours
- meals produced
- throughput
- remakes
- waste
- accuracy
- prep completion
- cleanup time

Later, team bonuses can reward throughput only when quality, safety, accuracy and waste gates also pass. Never reward speed alone.

Favor a small cross-trained crew and stagger staffing so prep staffing does not automatically equal rush staffing.

### Snell pricing currently used in planning
- 10 hr: $375/mo
- 20 hr: $710/mo
- 40 hr: $1,350/mo
- Full Time unlimited: $2,370/mo
- onboarding/admin: $500 one-time

Important pricing logic:
- 20-hour plan + overages remains cheaper than 40-hour plan until about 41-42 hours/month.
- Unlimited becomes economically attractive at roughly 74+ hours/month relative to 40-hour + overage.

### Current recommended starting tier
If Snell clears the hard gates, start with **20 hours/month**, not 40 and not unlimited. The 20-hour tier includes useful refrigerated storage and keeps startup commitment low.

### Snell hard gates before money changes hands
1. **HALAL certifier preflight passes** for the shared-kitchen arrangement.
2. Raw chicken/beef/shrimp receiving, prep, storage, cooking/cooling/reheating processes are allowed.
3. **CKT wok solution passes**: either a proper high-output wok burner is allowed/available, or the strongest permitted setup physically produces acceptable CKT.
4. Reliable access to required equipment during weekday lunch-production windows: wok/range + fryer + griddle + storage.
5. Total all-in cost/contract/storage rules match the advertised economics.

If any gate fails, do not force Snell to work just because it is cheap.

### Backup kitchens
1. **California Cookery** — relevant because it advertises shared and private/pod stations, fryer, griddle, range, storage and 24/7 access.
2. **iKitchens** — relevant because starting packages are inexpensive and they explicitly discuss specialized/custom equipment; potentially useful if we need a custom wok solution.

CloudKitchens/private ghost-kitchen suites were investigated and are currently rejected as too expensive for this stage (planning range roughly ~$4.5k-$6k+/mo in San Jose).

Standalone micro-commercial/second-generation space is a later-stage option only if it is already food-capable: raw meat permitted, existing Type I hood/suppression, grease/plumbing/gas, and all-in occupancy ideally <=~$3.5k-$4.5k. Avoid raw shells/buildout projects.
