# DAMB GOOD LAH — Kitchen Operating Layout v0.1

## Objective

Design the smallest production cell that can repeatedly produce the DAMB GOOD LAH launch menu at target quality and throughput without cross-traffic, raw-to-ready contamination risk, or unnecessary equipment duplication.

This is a **requirements document**, not a claim about The Commissary's exact internal floorplan. The Commissary currently advertises a 3,600 sq ft shared commercial kitchen with prep tables, range, fryer, griddle, ovens and cold/freezer/dry storage, but does not publicly disclose station dimensions, equipment counts, burner output or exact station geometry.

Primary design target:
- 20–30 meal launch runs with 2 people.
- 30–50 meal corporate runs with 3 people.
- 18+ main-equivalents / paid kitchen hour consumer.
- 24+ main-equivalents / paid kitchen hour corporate constrained menu.
- CKT remains protected and may have lower dedicated capacity than the rest of the menu.

---

# 1. Required operating footprint

## Active production cell

Target approximately **250–300 sq ft of contiguous working area**, plus shared walk-in storage and wash/sanitation access.

A practical shape is approximately:
- 12–14 ft wide x 20–22 ft long, or equivalent.
- 42–48 in primary aisle where two people must pass.
- minimum ~36 in secondary clearance where code/facility configuration permits.

The goal is adjacency, not raw square footage. A larger space with prep tables far from the hood is worse than a compact cell with correct flow.

## One-way product flow

**Receiving/storage → raw/cold prep → batch prep → hot line → finishing/assembly → pack/label → dispatch.**

Raw product should never need to cross the clean packing lane once production begins.

---

# 2. Hot line — launch requirement

## Wok positions: TWO

### Wok 1 — protected CKT position
- dedicated commercial high-output wok setup under approved Type I hood/fire suppression.
- used primarily for Char Kway Teow.
- dedicated wok/ring/smallwares if required by HALAL controls.

### Wok 2 — flexible noodle position
Used for:
- Damb Good Mee Goreng.
- dry curry noodle finishing.
- overflow / test work.

### Why two
One wok can make good food, but a mixed lunch run becomes serial:
CKT blocks Mee Goreng, or Mee Goreng blocks CKT.
Two positions allow one protected CKT lane while a second operator or the same operator in sequence handles the other noodle lane.

### Third wok
**Not required for launch.** Add only if measured 50+ meal demand shows the wok line is the actual bottleneck and the facility/hood can support it.

---

## Range: ONE commercial range

Minimum useful configuration:
- 4–6 burners.

Primary jobs:
- Padang curry.
- beef braise / reheat.
- chicken batch finishing.
- sambal.
- noodle blanch water.
- sauce reductions.

If only four burners are available, production sequencing must be validated before launch.

---

## Fryer: ONE fryer, preferably twin-basket

One fryer is enough **if crisp hitters are produced before service**.

Primary jobs:
- chicken-skin crisp development/production where fryer method wins.
- potato crackle.
- curry tofu crisp.
- ikan bilis.
- occasional other pre-service crisp work.

Do not build the menu around simultaneous service-time frying. The crisp-hitter program is specifically intended to move fryer demand upstream.

HALAL requirement remains unresolved: HFSAA must confirm whether dedicated fryer/oil is required in the shared facility.

---

## Griddle: ONE 36–48 in griddle position

Primary jobs:
- roti finishing.
- egg work if the final Nasi Lemak specification uses griddle eggs.
- controlled warming/finishing tasks.

A second griddle is not justified at launch.

---

## Rice cookers: TWO commercial units

Recommended architecture:
- Rice Cooker A: plain jasmine rice for Chicken Rice / Beef Rice.
- Rice Cooker B: pandan-coconut rice for Nasi Lemak.

Two independent units protect flavor identity and remove a hidden batch bottleneck.

For launch-scale 20–50 meal runs, two large commercial electric rice cookers should be sufficient if batch capacity is validated. A smaller backup is useful but not required in the rented active footprint.

---

## Hot hold / staging

Target:
- one 4-pan-equivalent hot-hold zone or equivalent Cambro/oven staging arrangement.

Likely contents during assembly:
- cooked chicken.
- beef.
- Padang curry.
- rice / coconut rice as needed.

This should sit immediately behind or beside assembly, not back at the cooking line.

# 3. Prep surfaces

## Prep Station A — RAW PROTEIN

**One 6-ft stainless table** preferred.

Jobs:
- raw halal chicken.
- raw halal beef.
- raw shrimp.
- lap cheong portioning if required.

Controls:
- dedicated color-coded boards/tools.
- raw product remains physically separated from garnish/packing.
- ideally located nearest approved prep sink / wash path and cold storage.

Once raw prep is complete, station is fully cleaned/sanitized and does not become the primary clean assembly station in the same rush unless the written SOP specifically validates that reset.

## Prep Station B — AROMATICS / VEGETABLES / COMPONENTS

**One 6-ft stainless table.**

Jobs:
- shallot / garlic / ginger / galangal / lemongrass packs.
- chile mother prep.
- sprouts/chives.
- potatoes.
- tofu.
- pickles.
- garnishes.
- sauce/component scaling.

This is the core Recipe Universe prep table.

## Packing/assembly station — CLEAN

**8–12 linear ft clean table space.**

Best configuration:
- two 6-ft tables end-to-end or in a shallow L.

Needs room for:
- containers/lids.
- rice portioning.
- protein/sauce pans.
- cold garnish bins.
- dry crisp hitters.
- labels/order sheets.
- bagging / grouped corporate order assembly.

For 20-meal runs one 6–8 ft table may survive. For 30–50 meal runs, **12 ft of clean linear assembly surface is strongly preferred**.

# 4. Cold storage requirement

The current 20-hour Snell tier includes:
- one 4-ft dry shelf.
- one 4-ft walk-in refrigerator shelf.
- no included freezer shelf.

That is probably too tight for DAMB GOOD LAH once real recurring production begins.

## Launch minimum — 20–30 meals

### Refrigerator
- **1 full 4-ft shelf**: ready-to-eat / sauces / produce / cooked components.
- **+ 1/2 shelf raw-protein zone**: thawing raw chicken/beef/shrimp below/segregated as facility and food-safety rules require.

### Freezer
- **+ 1/2 freezer shelf** minimum for frozen chicken/shrimp/lap cheong reserve.

This means at least **+$120/month** over the published 20-hour tier if one half refrigerator shelf and one half freezer shelf are sufficient.

## Preferred — 30–50 meal recurring production

- 1 full 4-ft RTE/component refrigerator shelf.
- 1 full 4-ft raw-protein refrigerator shelf or equivalent clearly segregated allocation.
- 1/2 to 1 full freezer shelf depending delivery frequency and case size.

Budget roughly **+$180 to +$240/month** of storage beyond the base tier if the exact shelf geometry requires a full extra refrigerator allocation and freezer capacity.

Do not finalize this until HFSAA states the required physical segregation.

---

# 5. Dry storage

## Included 4-ft dry shelf

Reserve it primarily for food-critical inventory:
- rice.
- coconut milk.
- approved sauces.
- dried chile.
- oil.
- peanuts / ikan bilis where appropriate.
- dry seasonings.

## Packaging

At launch, bulky case packaging should remain **offsite whenever practical** and be brought in by shift, rather than consuming expensive commissary shelving.

Add a paid half dry shelf only if repeated setup labor becomes more expensive than storage.

## Dedicated HALAL smallwares

Use one clearly labeled mobile rack/bin system for:
- woks.
- hotel pans.
- Cambros.
- utensils.
- cutting boards.
- fryer tools.
- sanitation-controlled smallwares.

Exact storage method must follow certifier requirements.

---

# 6. Dispatch / staging

Need approximately **4–6 linear ft** of clean staging or one mobile speed rack/cart for:
- completed labeled meals.
- corporate-order grouping.
- insulated carriers.
- courier pickup.

Packed food should flow away from raw prep and dishwashing traffic.

# 7. Preferred physical arrangement

```text
        WALK-IN / DRY STORAGE
                 |
                 v
+----------------------------------------------+
| RAW PREP 6' | AROMATIC / COMPONENT PREP 6'  |
+----------------------------------------------+
                  |
                  v
================================================
 TYPE I HOOD / HOT LINE
 [RANGE] [WOK 1 CKT] [WOK 2 FLEX] [FRYER] [GRIDDLE]
================================================
                  |
                  v
     [RICE A] [RICE B] [HOT HOLD]
                  |
                  v
+----------------------------------------------+
|      CLEAN ASSEMBLY / PACKING 8–12'          |
| rice -> protein -> sauce -> garnish -> crisp |
+----------------------------------------------+
                  |
                  v
        LABEL / BAG / DISPATCH RACK
                  |
                  v
                 EXIT

Wash / dish / hand sinks remain off the main forward food-flow lane.
```

The key design rule is that the cook should be able to pivot from wok/range to landing area without walking across the kitchen, while the assembler can build and close meals without entering the hot line.

# 8. Staffing by scale

## 20–30 meals — TWO PEOPLE

### Person A — hot line
- woks.
- range.
- final noodle work.
- hot components.

### Person B — assembly / support
- rice.
- fryer/griddle as scheduled.
- garnish.
- portioning.
- pack/label.

Both do early prep before rush.

## 30–50 meals — THREE PEOPLE

### Person A — wok lead
- CKT + Mee Goreng sequence.

### Person B — batch/hot support
- range.
- rice.
- fryer/griddle.
- hot hold replenishment.

### Person C — assembly/packing lead
- box build.
- garnish/crisps.
- labels.
- order grouping.

At this point the assembly line, not a third wok, may become the larger bottleneck. Measure before adding equipment.

# 10. What would make Snell fail the layout test

Reject or redesign if any of these are true:

1. Only one usable hot station can be reserved at a time.
2. No approved commercial wok solution can be placed under the hood.
3. Two wok positions cannot operate without blocking range/fryer access and CKT becomes chronically serial.
4. Prep station is far enough from the hot line that cooks repeatedly walk across shared traffic.
5. Clean assembly space is <~8 linear ft for corporate runs.
6. Raw prep and packing cannot be physically separated.
7. Walk-in allocations cannot satisfy HFSAA/food-safety segregation.
8. 20-hour members cannot reliably reserve simultaneous hot line + prep + packing area during weekday production.
9. Dishwashing/shared-tenant traffic cuts directly through the clean assembly/dispatch lane.

# 11. Tour measurements to capture

Before committing, measure/photograph:

- hood linear footage.
- each existing hot appliance width.
- exact range burner count and output/model.
- whether two wok rings can physically fit under approved hood coverage.
- prep table count and dimensions.
- distance from prep tables to walk-in and hot line.
- aisle widths.
- location/count of hand sinks and prep sinks.
- dishwashing traffic path.
- exact refrigerator/freezer shelf dimensions and vertical clearance.
- whether shelves are fixed or user-configurable.
- clean landing space immediately beside wok line.
- available electrical circuits/outlets for two commercial rice cookers.
- receiving entrance and route to walk-in.
- dispatch/courier pickup route.

Photograph the station from all four corners and the inside of allocated storage before signing.

# 12. Working recommendation

For DAMB GOOD LAH launch, the target kitchen is **not** a giant restaurant kitchen. It is a disciplined compact production cell with:

- **2 wok positions**.
- **1 range**.
- **1 fryer**.
- **1 griddle**.
- **2 rice cookers**.
- **2 six-foot prep tables**.
- **8–12 ft clean assembly/packing**.
- **1 hot-hold zone**.
- **1 dispatch rack**.
- **~1.5 refrigerator shelves + 0.5 freezer shelf at minimum**.
- **~250–300 sq ft contiguous active footprint** plus shared cold storage and sinks.

The layout should support one-direction flow and two-person production without collision. Add equipment only when measured 30/50-meal runs identify a real bottleneck.
