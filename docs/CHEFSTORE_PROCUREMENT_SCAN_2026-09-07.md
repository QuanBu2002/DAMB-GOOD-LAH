4. a source for low-risk commodity items even when proteins come from another vendor.

# Shrimp decision — likely sweet spot is 26/30

For CKT, the strongest apples-to-apples current peeled/deveined path is Aqua Star:

- 26/30: **$6.71/lb**
- 21/25: **$7.35/lb**
- 16/20: **$8.11/lb**

At the current development target of roughly 70–80 g shrimp per CKT, the 26/30 size is economically attractive and should be the default sensory-test candidate.

Do not assume larger shrimp are better. Run blind CKT tests at equal edible portion weight and compare:

- visual abundance
- bite distribution
- wok handling
- 0/15/30-minute texture
- perceived value

The 26/30 spec wins if customers do not meaningfully prefer the larger sizes.

The Searock 26/30 price of roughly **$5.87/lb** is even more attractive, but usable yield/specification must be confirmed because the public product title does not establish peeled/deveined status. A cheaper shell-on or less-trimmed shrimp may be a false economy after labor and yield.

# Packaging opportunity

The 32 oz Eco-Systems container/lid combos are materially below the existing ~$0.65 corporate packaging target:

- round: **~$0.21 each**
- rectangle: **~$0.24 each**

This is important because it leaves roughly **$0.40+ per corporate meal** for:

- labels
- grouped outer carrier
- optional utensils/napkins
- sauce cup when required
- tamper element when required

without automatically exceeding the current ~$0.65 corporate packaging target.

Do not declare victory until 0/15/30-minute tests establish:

- curry leakage
- steam/condensation
- noodle texture
- crispy garnish protection
- stacking
- transport stability
- microwave compatibility

If the rectangle performs well, it may become the default main container because presentation and packing density may be better for rice/noodle meals.

# Coconut milk decision — unit price vs waste

The large 98 oz Chaokoh case is cheaper per ounce than the 13.5 oz cans:

- 98 oz case: **~$0.119/oz**
- 13.5 oz case: **~$0.133/oz**

Only about a 10% unit-cost difference.

Therefore volume determines the correct pack size:

- pilot / low volume: smaller cans may actually have lower **usable** cost if the large can creates spoilage/waste;
- stabilized prep: move to 98 oz cans once a full can can be consumed safely within the validated production/storage window.

This is exactly the project's rule: compare landed **usable** cost, not sticker price.

Koch halal thigh remains the single highest-value qualification target, but it is not approved until:

1. exact current halal certificate is obtained;
2. certifier/slaughter standard is known;
3. chosen DAMB GOOD LAH certifier accepts the exact SKU;
4. San Jose stock and current price are confirmed;
5. cooked yield + flavor pass against backup product.

# Procurement architecture recommendation

CHEF'STORE should become a **benchmark-and-pickup lane**, not a sole supplier.

### Likely CHEF'STORE primary buys if tests pass

- chicken thigh candidate
- eggs
- frying oil
- rice
- potatoes
- coconut milk
- shrimp
- packaging

### Keep delivered-wholesale redundancy for

- halal chicken
- beef
- shrimp at higher volume
- produce where frequent delivery reduces labor
- specialty Malaysian/Thai pantry
- certified halal chicken lap cheong

### Rule

A CHEF'STORE item wins only when:

**pickup-adjusted landed cost + labor + waste + quality + certification**

beats the best delivered alternative.

Do not save $10 on a case by spending $25 of labor/time making an isolated procurement run. Batch pickup into a scheduled weekly procurement route or use Click&Carry when the cart is already >$100.
