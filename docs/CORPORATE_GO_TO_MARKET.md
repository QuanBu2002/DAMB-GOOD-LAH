# DAMB GOOD LAH — Office / Corporate Drop Strategy

## Thesis

The low consumer menu price is supported by **dense, predictable orders**, not by accepting thin economics on one delivery-app order at a time.

A 20-40 meal office order can replace many individual transactions while improving:
- kitchen batching
- demand predictability
- waste
- packaging
- delivery allocation
- payment/admin overhead
- customer acquisition
- repeat-order potential

## Target customers

Prioritize recurring groups with a coordinator who can reorder:
- tech offices
- startups
- hospitals / clinics
- university departments
- research labs
- professional offices
- conferences / meetings
- community organizations
- medical practices
- coworking spaces

HALAL certification materially improves the organizer's ability to choose DAMB GOOD LAH for mixed groups without excluding Muslim colleagues.

## Office menu design

Day-1 office menu can use the same launch dishes, but larger orders should be encouraged toward **2-3 main choices** rather than six equal micro-batches.

Best early office candidates:
- Padang Chicken Rice
- Nasi Lemak Lah
- Padang Beef Rice
- Damb Good Mee Goreng
- Roti / Padang dip

CKT should remain available selectively after the wok station proves corporate-volume quality; do not bulk-cook CKT so far ahead that wok character collapses.

## Order-size architecture

- <10 meals: normal direct consumer/group order
- 10-19 meals: office drop, limited discounting
- **20-39 meals: preferred target**
- 40+ meals: catering workflow; menu concentration and delivery timing agreed in advance

The project should not automatically discount corporate food. A discount is earned only by genuine operational savings: larger order size, earlier preorder, fewer dish types, simpler packaging or pickup/dense delivery.

## Pricing logic

Consumer anchors:
- Chicken Rice / Mee Goreng: $11.95
- Nasi Lemak / Curry Mee: $12.95
- Beef Rice / CKT: $14.95

Corporate pricing should be modeled as **realized average price**, not a universal catering discount. The v0.2 economics model currently uses a base ~$12.75 realized price per corporate meal.

## Base corporate economic target

Current planning case for a 20-meal drop:
- ~$12.75 average realized meal price
- ~$4.25 food + waste
- ~$0.65 packaging / meal
- 24 main-equivalents / total paid kitchen hour
- two kitchen workers
- ~$35.50 kitchen cost / hour
- ~$12 delivery / drop
- ~2.5% payment/admin leakage
- one product-cost office-captain meal

This produces roughly **$2.74 contribution per paid meal / ~$55 per 20-meal drop** before fixed overhead. This is a planning model, not measured performance.

The economics should improve with:
- 30-50 meal drops
- higher constrained-menu productivity
- wholesale procurement
- lower packaging
- route clustering
- repeat accounts

## Office captain program

Use a person inside the organization as the recurring acquisition channel.

Potential reward after a minimum qualifying order:
- organizer meal
- roti for the table
- small direct-order credit

Value the reward at **incremental product cost**, not menu price. A $3-$5 product-cost reward can be a rational acquisition cost for a $150-$500 recurring order.

Do not give broad cash discounts without an order threshold.

## Delivery strategy

- One office address should have one coordinated delivery/handoff.
- Cluster nearby office drops into planned routes where possible.
- Measure actual driver time, mileage/courier price, waiting and parking.
- Use a clear handoff person / phone number.
- Avoid the chaos of 20 independent app drivers for 20 meals.

## Corporate packaging

Develop a second packaging system instead of forcing consumer delivery packaging onto catering.

Target base: **~$0.65 per meal**, subject to delivery-quality tests.

Options to test:
- individually boxed mains + grouped outer carriers
- consolidated utensils/napkins by request
- shared roti carrier where quality permits
- grouped sealed sauce/dip handling where appropriate
- minimal redundant outer bags/labels

Do not sacrifice food safety, allergen communication or delivery quality merely to hit a package-cost target.

## Sales outreach

### Direct outbound
Build a lightweight CRM of:
- office manager / workplace experience
- executive assistant
- operations lead
- department administrator
- lab manager
- clinic/practice manager
- event coordinator

Initial offer should make ordering easy rather than over-discounted:
- simple office menu
- HALAL status/certification once approved
- minimum order
- delivery zone/window
- one contact
- first-drop table roti or organizer incentive

### Social
Use TikTok, Instagram and Facebook for legitimate automated content workflows:
- batch video editing
- captions
- scheduled posting
- creative variants
- geographic ad testing
- CRM follow-up
- reporting

Do not use fake followers, fake comments, fake likes or engagement manipulation. Automate labor, not authenticity.

## Employee incentive system

A performance bonus should require **all gates**, not speed alone:
1. economic productivity target
2. quality/taste/QC pass
3. order accuracy
4. waste target
5. safety / temperature-control compliance
6. station reset / cleanup

Example planning pool: **$20-$50 per qualifying shift**. The bonus is good economics only when incremental contribution exceeds the bonus and payroll treatment is compliant.

## Metrics to own

For every office order record:
- meals/drop
- realized $/meal
- menu mix
- number of distinct mains
- paid kitchen hours
- main-equiv/paid hour
- food waste
- packaging $/meal
- delivery $/meal
- order errors/remakes
- on-time delivery
- customer acquisition source
- repeat interval
- contribution/drop
- organizer contact / next follow-up

## Stage gate

Before calling corporate the economic engine, run at least:
1. one 10-meal internal simulation
2. two 20-meal simulations
3. one 30+ meal simulation
4. multiple real paid drops with repeat customers

The business model is validated by **repeatable contribution and repeat ordering**, not merely by successfully cooking one large lunch.
