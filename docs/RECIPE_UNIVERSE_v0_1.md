# DAMB GOOD LAH — Recipe Universe v0.1

## Purpose

Build a **flavor-dense, low-waste, high-throughput production universe** in which ingredients and trim streams overlap intelligently without collapsing the four protected flavor lanes.

The design target is not “one sauce everywhere.” It is:

> **Shared raw materials and neutral mother-preps; distinct finished flavors.**

Every prep should do at least one of the following:
1. serve multiple dishes;
2. create a signature sensory payoff;
3. convert trim/byproduct into useful flavor;
4. reduce service-time labor;
5. improve delivery stability;
6. reduce waste or procurement complexity.

If a prep does none of these, delete it.

---

# 1. Protected flavor lanes

These remain non-negotiably distinct.

## Lane A — PADANG
Signature: aromatic spice paste, coconut richness, chile, warm spice, slow-developed savory depth.

Used by:
- Padang Chicken Rice
- Padang Beef Rice
- Padang Dry Curry Mee
- Roti + Padang curry dip

Can share raw aromatics with other lanes, but the finished Padang rempah/curry is protected.

## Lane B — HOUSE SAMBAL
Signature: chile-forward, fried aromatic depth, sweet-sour-salty balance, seafood umami where appropriate.

Used by:
- Nasi Lemak Lah
- future Sambal Shrimp Rice
- future Sambal Shrimp Mee

The base can branch into dish-specific finishes; it must not simply become CKT chile paste or Mee Goreng sauce.

## Lane C — MAMAK MEE GORENG
Signature: savory-sweet-tangy-red sauce, wok-cooked yellow noodles, potato, tofu, egg, sprouts.

Used by:
- Damb Good Mee Goreng

The sauce remains dedicated even if it draws on shared chile puree, tamarind, soy-family condiments or garlic.

## Lane D — CHAR KWAY TEOW
Signature: high heat, fresh flat rice noodles, animal-fat richness, garlic, prawn, halal chicken lap cheong, egg, sprouts, chives, dark/light soy seasoning and dedicated chile.

Used by:
- Char Kway Teow only at launch

CKT is the benchmark dish. No Padang curry, house sambal or generic noodle sauce substitutes.

# 3. Neutral mother-preps

These are where efficiency should happen.

## MP-01 — Dried chile mother puree

Neutral soaked/blended dried chile with no lane-specific seasoning.

Branches into:
- house sambal
- Mee Goreng red sauce
- CKT chile paste
- Padang rempah where appropriate

Rule: **never hold one finished chile sauce and dilute/reseason it into four dishes.** Share the mechanical prep; separate the cooking and finishing.

## MP-02 — Aromatic mise en place

Central batch prep of:
- peeled shallots
- peeled garlic
- ginger
- galangal
- trimmed lemongrass

These can be weighed into lane-specific rempah packs.

Goal: one washing/peeling/processor setup, four different formulas.

## MP-03 — Tamarind concentrate

One measured, filtered tamarind extraction.

Potential uses:
- house sambal acid
- Mee Goreng tang
- selected Padang balance adjustment

Do not force into CKT unless sensory testing justifies it.

## MP-04 — Coconut milk staging

Same approved coconut-milk SKU can feed:
- Padang curry
- nasi lemak coconut rice

The finished preparations remain separate.

## MP-05 — Garlic service prep

One peeled/chopped garlic workflow can support:
- CKT
- Mee Goreng
- sambal
- Padang rempah

Final chop size and cooking treatment may differ by lane.

# 4. Protein cascade system

## CHICKEN CASCADE

### Primary product
Chicken thigh meat →
- Padang Chicken Rice
- Nasi Lemak Lah
- Padang Dry Curry Mee

One base chicken preparation remains the operating target, with dish-specific finishing.

### Chicken skin
If economically sourced separately or generated from a skin-on specification:
1. render / crisp skin;
2. use crisp on Padang Chicken Rice;
3. capture rendered chicken fat.

### Rendered chicken fat — high-value R&D lane
Potential controlled uses:
- **CKT cooking fat** to restore halal animal-fat richness historically supplied by lard;
- Padang Chicken Rice finishing;
- selected fried-aromatic production.

Do not automatically use it everywhere. CKT fat should be sensory-tested against neutral oil.

### Important procurement tension
The current low-cost Koch candidate is **boneless skinless thigh**. That means chicken-skin crisp cannot be assumed to be a free byproduct. We need one of three solutions:
- obtain inexpensive halal chicken skin separately;
- periodically buy a skin-on cut whose total yield economics beat buying skin separately;
- preserve the crisp as a signature garnish only if its incremental cost earns its sensory value.

This must be measured before locking chicken procurement.

---

## BEEF CASCADE

### Primary product
Shoulder clod / chuck / best-yield braising cut → Padang Beef Rice.

### Fat trim
Trim intentionally before braise where appropriate → render slowly.

Outputs:
- clean beef fat
- crisped trim solids where safe/useful

### Rendered beef fat
Primary protected use:
- **beef-fat shallot crisp** for Padang Beef Rice.

Secondary R&D use:
- enrich beef braise/rempah if sensory testing benefits.

Do not spread beef fat into unrelated dishes merely because it exists.

---

## SHRIMP CASCADE — DEFERRED UNTIL SHELL-ON WINS ECONOMICALLY

At current CHEF'STORE pricing, peeled/deveined shrimp is the operational launch default because shell-on savings do not clearly beat edible-yield loss and labor.

However, when shell-on shrimp drops below our buy threshold, use the whole stream:

### Shrimp meat
- CKT
- future Sambal Shrimp Rice
- future Sambal Shrimp Mee
- future shrimp wonton

### Shrimp shells / tails
R&D paths:

#### A. Prawn oil
Toast/fry shells in approved oil, strain and standardize.
Potential uses:
- CKT finishing fat or chile paste
- Sambal Shrimp finishing oil
- future seafood noodle specials

#### B. Concentrated prawn stock
Roast/sweat shells, extract, strain, reduce under controlled food-safety process.
Potential uses:
- shrimp sambal depth
- CKT seasoning micro-dose
- future seafood curry / laksa-like development if soups are ever reconsidered outside the Day-1 menu

#### C. Shell powder — experimental only
Dry/toast/grind only if food-safety, texture and allergen control are excellent. Lower priority than oil/stock.

Rule: shell utilization becomes commercial only after measuring labor, yield, storage burden, allergen implications and sensory improvement.

# 5. Vegetable / starch cascade system

## POTATO

One potato prep, multiple outputs:

1. cooked potato body → Damb Good Mee Goreng
2. selected portion → dry/chill/crisp → Mee Goreng crispy potato garnish
3. trim → candidate mash/binder/R&D only if useful; otherwise discard/compost rather than create complexity

Goal: no separate potato SKU or completely separate prep process for garnish.

## TOFU

One firm tofu SKU:

1. tender/fried cubes → Mee Goreng body
2. pressed / seasoned / crisped → curry tofu crisp on Padang Dry Curry Mee

Same procurement, two textures.

## BEAN SPROUTS

Shared across:
- Mee Goreng
- Padang Dry Curry Mee
- CKT

Because sprouts deteriorate quickly, they are a high-priority demand-forecast/waste-control SKU.

## LIME

Shared across:
- Mee Goreng finish
- future Sambal Shrimp Mee
- possible CKT optional finish only if benchmark testing supports it

Use zest only when it improves a product; do not invent low-value uses merely to eliminate trim.

# 6. Ikan bilis + peanut system

Protected primarily to Nasi Lemak because peanuts introduce a major allergen lane.

## Ikan bilis
Primary:
- ikan bilis-peanut crunch

Potential secondary:
- clean ikan bilis crumbs/fines into Nasi Lemak sambal seasoning if texture and salt can be standardized
- strained frying oil reused **only within the approved Nasi Lemak/ikan-bilis lane** if quality and food-safety standards permit

## Peanuts
Keep geographically and operationally constrained.

Do **not** spread peanut into other dishes merely for cross-utilization; minimizing allergen surface area is more valuable than forcing ingredient overlap.

# 7. Shallot system

Shallot is one of the highest-value overlapping aromatics.

Central purchasing + peeling feeds:
- Padang rempah
- house sambal
- CKT chile paste if formula uses shallot
- fried shallot garnishes
- beef-fat shallot crisp

### Fried shallot logic
One raw shallot SKU, but two possible finishing fats:
- neutral/chicken-fat fried shallot for general use where appropriate
- beef-fat shallot crisp reserved for Padang Beef Rice

Do not pre-mix these outputs.

# 8. Chile system

## Input ladder
- dried red chile
- fresh red chile
- optional hotter chile for controlled heat

## Branches

### Padang chile component
Integrated into spice paste and coconut curry.

### House sambal
Deeply fried with its own aromatic/umami/acid/sugar architecture.

### Mee Goreng chile component
Supports a dedicated Mamak sauce with soy/sweet/tangy profile.

### CKT chile paste
Lean, sharp, wok-friendly chile paste designed to hit the hot wok without flooding it.

Shared chile procurement. Four distinct finished products.

# 9. Sauce family tree

## HOUSE PADANG CURRY
Mother outputs:
- full curry for Chicken Rice
- full curry for Beef Rice
- reduced/drier coating format for Curry Mee
- dip-consistency format for Roti + Padang dip

This is **one lane with controlled concentration states**, not four unrelated curries.

Critical R&D question: can one master Padang curry batch be split into service-consistency branches without sacrificing chicken/beef/noodle identity?

## HOUSE SAMBAL
Potential architecture:
- master cooked sambal
  - Nasi Lemak finish
  - future shrimp sambal + prawn oil/stock finish
  - future noodle sambal + charred lime finish

## MEE GORENG SAUCE
Dedicated finished sauce.
Can share raw chile puree, tamarind, soy-family ingredients and sugar with inventory, but final sauce is independent.

## CKT SEASONING
Dedicated light/dark soy + approved savory seasoning + sugar/pepper architecture, portioned for very fast wok dosing.

CKT chile remains separate so heat level can be controlled without changing salt/sugar balance.

High-heat Malaysian references consistently support fresh flat rice noodles, prawns, egg, sprouts/chives and distinct soy/chile seasoning; this reinforces keeping CKT as a separate station formula rather than a generic noodle sauce.

# 12. Prep architecture

## PREP BLOCK A — aromatics
- peel shallots
- peel garlic
- prep ginger/galangal/lemongrass
- soak/blend dried chile
- make tamarind concentrate
- weigh lane-specific rempah packs

## PREP BLOCK B — proteins
- portion chicken
- recover skin/fat if present
- trim beef; segregate fat
- portion shrimp
- process shells only when shell-on program is active

## PREP BLOCK C — sauces
- cook Padang curry
- cook house sambal
- cook Mee Goreng sauce
- mix CKT seasoning
- cook CKT chile paste

Keep each vessel/label/color code distinct despite shared upstream prep.

## PREP BLOCK D — texture components
- chicken-skin crisp
- beef-fat shallot crisp
- ikan bilis-peanut crunch
- crispy potato
- curry tofu crisp

Most should be completed before service wherever food-safety and texture testing permit.

## PREP BLOCK E — starches
- rice
- coconut rice
- shared potato
- yellow-noodle staging
- fresh-flat-rice-noodle staging only near service

# 13. What we should NOT cross-utilize

Efficiency has limits.

Do not merge:
- Padang curry and house sambal
- house sambal and CKT chile paste
- Mee Goreng sauce and CKT seasoning
- yellow noodles and CKT fresh flat noodles
- beef fat into unrelated dishes
- peanut products across the whole menu
- shrimp-shell extracts into non-seafood dishes without explicit sensory/allergen rationale
- generic sausage into CKT

Do not add a byproduct use merely because it exists. A waste stream must improve flavor or economics enough to justify storage, labeling, labor and risk.

# 14. R&D experiments — ranked

## Tier 1 — highest-value

### RU-01 Chicken-fat CKT
Compare:
A. neutral canola
B. 50/50 neutral + rendered chicken fat
C. rendered chicken fat dominant

Measure:
- wok aroma
- noodle gloss
- perceived richness
- lap-cheong integration
- 15/30-minute delivery quality
- cost

### RU-02 Padang concentration tree
Make one master curry and split into:
- rice curry
- dry curry mee coating
- roti dip

Determine whether one mother batch can truly support all three without compromise.

### RU-03 One chicken prep, three dishes
Same core chicken batch finished for:
- Chicken Rice
- Nasi Lemak
- Curry Mee

Goal: prove operational sharing without making the dishes taste identical.

### RU-04 Potato two-output prep
One potato cook →
- Mee Goreng body
- crisp garnish

Measure labor and texture.

### RU-05 Tofu two-output prep
One firm tofu SKU → tender body + crisp garnish.

## Tier 2

### RU-06 Beef-fat shallot yield
Measure fat trim from candidate beef cuts and whether it supplies enough fat for garnish without supplemental purchase.

### RU-07 House chile mother
Test whether one neutral dried-chile puree can feed Padang, sambal, Mee Goreng and CKT without quality loss.

### RU-08 Coconut milk utilization
Track one opened bulk can across Padang + nasi lemak production to find the volume at which bulk cans beat smaller cans after spoilage.

## Tier 3 — activate when shell-on shrimp becomes economical

### RU-09 Prawn oil
Shells → infused oil → CKT / shrimp sambal sensory test.

### RU-10 Prawn stock concentrate
Shells → concentrated extract → shrimp sambal / CKT micro-dose.

Do not activate shell-stream production until shell-on procurement plus labor makes economic sense.

# 15. Recipe-universe scorecard

Every proposed module gets scored 0–2 on:

- flavor impact
- dishes served
- labor saved
- waste reduced
- delivery stability
- cost impact
- inventory reduction

Then subtract 0–2 each for:
- food-safety complexity
- HALAL-certification complexity
- allergen spread
- storage burden
- service-time burden

A module with weak net value does not enter the commercial universe.

---

# 16. The target end-state

DAMB GOOD LAH should feel like a menu of distinct dishes to the customer but operate backstage like a tightly engineered flavor network.

The ideal architecture is:

- one chicken family
- one beef family
- one shrimp family
- one yellow-noodle family
- protected fresh CKT noodle
- one tofu SKU
- one potato SKU
- shared aromatics
- one neutral chile mother
- one tamarind concentrate
- one coconut-milk inventory lane
- four protected finished flavor lanes
- byproducts converted into signature fats, crisps and seafood flavor only when the math and taste justify them

**The kitchen should reuse ingredients, not repeat flavors.**
