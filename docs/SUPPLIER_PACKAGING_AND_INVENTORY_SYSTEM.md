# DAMB GOOD LAH — Supplier, Packaging + Inventory System

## Objective

Create a sourcing system that is authentic, HALAL-verifiable, economically disciplined and resilient without tying up excessive cash in inventory.

---

# 1. Supplier qualification standard

No supplier is "approved" only because it sells the right category.

Record for every critical item:
- vendor.
- exact manufacturer/brand.
- exact SKU.
- case pack.
- unit size.
- origin.
- current certifier/certificate if relevant.
- certificate expiration.
- ingredient statement.
- landed case cost.
- landed $/kg or usable unit.
- order minimum.
- delivery fee.
- order cutoff.
- lead time.
- delivery days.
- storage requirement.
- known alternate vendor.
- approved alternate SKU.
- last price-check date.

# 3. Protein cost discipline

Never compare suppliers using only raw $/lb.

Formula:

`landed usable cooked $/kg = (case price + freight + direct procurement cost) / usable cooked kg`

Example variables that can reverse the apparent winner:
- trim.
- fat/connective tissue.
- purge.
- cook loss.
- portion breakage.

For chicken thighs test at least one full representative batch from each serious source before locking supplier economics.

For beef, compare chuck/shoulder/clod options on actual braised yield and texture.

# 5. Lap-cheong program

## Acceptance specification

Product must be:
- genuine Chinese sausage / la chang / lap cheong profile.
- chicken-based halal product.
- current credible halal certification acceptable to chosen certifier.
- legally distributed/imported in the U.S.
- consistent restaurant-volume supply.
- sliceable thinly.
- able to render/caramelize at wok heat.
- sweet-savory cured profile that works with shrimp/egg/noodle seasoning.
- acceptable at 15/30 minute delivery.
- economically compatible with $14.95 CKT.

## Sourcing ladder

1. Bestt Impex.
2. CKK Paradiso.
3. identify Rabiah/equivalent manufacturer and U.S.-eligible importer.
4. alternate genuine certified halal chicken Chinese-sausage manufacturer.

Never descend the ladder into a frankfurter.

## Inventory policy once source is locked

Because lap cheong is a unique import-dependent critical ingredient, carry more safety stock than a commodity ingredient if shelf life permits.

Determine safety stock from:
- average weekly use.
- replenishment lead time.
- lead-time variance.
- import interruption risk.
- shelf life.
- cash cost.

# 6. Packaging test architecture

## Consumer candidates

Start by testing:
- 8x8 or 9x9 PP/clamshell for rice dishes.
- compartment vs single compartment where curry migration matters.
- separate small cup/bag only for crisp components where necessary.
- noodle package that permits enough steam escape without leakage.

Do not assume one container should serve all six mains.

### Likely architecture to test

Rice dishes:
- leak-resistant microwave-safe base.
- curry can contact rice only if sensory test says it improves rather than destroys texture.

Nasi lemak:
- compartmentalization may have more value because sambal, egg and crunch have different moisture characteristics.

Mee goreng/curry mee:
- vent/steam management more important than compartment count.

CKT:
- widest practical base, shallow fill, minimized steaming.

## Corporate

Prioritize:
- fast closure.
- stackability.
- 2–3 main visual labels.
- outer case/carrier.
- grouped utensil/napkin handling.

Target ~ $0.65/meal is a development target, not a requirement to buy poor packaging.

# 7. Packaging vendor strategy

Quote the **same test basket** from multiple vendors.

Basket example:
- 8x8 single PP.
- 8x8/9x9 3-compartment.
- 16/24/32 oz rectangular or round containers as needed.
- 2 oz portion cups/lids.
- kraft handled bag / catering bag.
- labels.
- foil catering pan/lid.
- gloves.

Compare:
- $/case.
- pieces/case.
- $/piece.
- minimum order.
- delivery fee.
- stock continuity.
- pickup distance.
- case storage volume.

A supplier that saves $0.05/container but forces large MOQs or frequent pickup labor may be worse economically.

# 8. Inventory categories

## A — critical unique
Failure stops a dish/menu promise.

Examples:
- halal lap cheong.
- certifier-approved unique sauce/blend.

Carry deliberate safety stock.

## B — critical but substitutable
Approved alternate exists.

Examples:
- jasmine rice.
- chicken.
- beef.
- coconut milk.
- yellow noodles.

Keep backup supplier active.

## C — commodity
Easy to replace within specification.

Examples:
- common produce.
- eggs.
- generic approved dry goods.

Do not overstock.

## D — packaging
Can stop sales despite food availability.

Maintain enough core package inventory for committed orders plus buffer.

---

# 9. Reorder system

For each item calculate:

`reorder point = expected use during lead time + safety stock`

Do not use arbitrary par levels indefinitely.

After four weeks of real sales, recompute from actual demand.

Unique imports should use higher lead-time uncertainty than local produce.

---

# 10. Receiving control

At receiving verify:
- supplier.
- SKU/brand.
- quantity.
- temperature where required.
- package integrity.
- lot/date where relevant.
- halal/certification status has not changed.
- invoice retained.

Reject silent substitutions from distributors unless the replacement SKU is already approved.

---

# 11. Supplier performance score

Monthly or after sufficient orders score 1–5:
- price.
- fill rate.
- on-time delivery.
- quality consistency.
- documentation.
- responsiveness.
- substitution discipline.
- minimum/order flexibility.

Switching suppliers is justified by total operating performance, not small sticker-price differences.

# 13. Cost-update rule

Never overwrite a planning price without retaining:
- old assumption.
- new quoted price.
- quote date.
- source/vendor.
- freight.
- pack size.
- whether price is public, verbal or written.
