# DAMB GOOD LAH — HALAL Architecture

## Principle

HALAL is a hard operating constraint, not a late-stage marketing sticker. The goal is a credible fully HALAL operation that preserves Malaysian culinary identity.

Use this rule when adapting dishes:

> **Look first at how Malaysian Muslims already solve the ingredient/process problem. Adapt only when the U.S. supply chain or chosen certifier requires it.**

## Locked culinary decisions

- No pork enters commercial DAMB GOOD LAH inventory or production.
- Char kway teow keeps Chinese sausage through **certified halal chicken lap cheong / la chang**.
- Generic halal chicken frankfurters are not an acceptable authenticity substitute.
- CKT remains a protected high-heat dish with fresh flat rice noodles, shrimp, egg, sprouts/chives, dedicated seasoning and chile paste.
- Mee goreng keeps its own Mamak-style sauce rather than being converted into a generic curry noodle.
- House sambal and Padang curry remain distinct lanes.

3. U.S. import/distribution is legal and commercially reliable.

8. Supplier has enough continuity for restaurant/catering volume.

## Ingredient-control matrix

### Meat + poultry
- Chicken: verified/certifier-acceptable halal source.
- Beef: verified/certifier-acceptable halal source.
- Lap cheong: certified halal chicken Chinese sausage.
- Shrimp/seafood: actual supplier/process spec reviewed as required by certifier.

### Processed ingredients that require SKU-level review

Do not infer halal status merely because an item appears vegetarian or seafood-based.

Review at minimum:
- belacan
- fish sauce
- light soy sauce
- dark soy sauce
- kecap manis
- ketchup / flavor systems if needed
- frying oil
- ghee
- commercial wrappers
- seasoning blends
- processing aids / flavor extracts where present

## Facility hard gate

Kitchen selection now has **two simultaneous non-negotiables**:

1. CKT-quality equipment: adequate wok burner output/recovery plus compatible griddle/fryer/rice/storage capacity.
2. Halal-control feasibility acceptable to the chosen certifier.

Do not sign a long-term kitchen commitment before certifier preflight of the actual site arrangement.

### Current Snell Avenue finding

The current leading shared-kitchen candidate, **The Commissary at 5475 Snell Ave, San Jose**, lists a fryer, griddle and range and currently lists $710/month for 20 reserved hours plus a $500 onboarding fee. Its public equipment list does **not** identify a dedicated high-BTU wok burner, so CKT still requires an in-person burner test.

A meal-prep business operating at the same address publicly states that it uses halal-certified poultry and beef; however, its own disclaimer says that halal certification applies specifically to those proteins. This is useful evidence that halal-certified meat is already handled at the site, but it is **not evidence that the shared facility itself satisfies DAMB GOOD LAH's intended full-HALAL control architecture**.

Therefore the sequence is:
1. tour/burner test;
2. document pork/non-halal production, fryer/oil, utensils and storage;
3. certifier preflight;
4. only then commit.

References:
- https://www.thekitchendoor.com/kitchen-rental/the-commissary-64552
- https://gainzandshredsmealprep.com/disclaimer/

Questions to resolve:

- What storage segregation is required?
- What receiving/labeling system is required?
- Are dedicated utensils/pans/wok tools required?

- What documentation/training is required?

## Certification candidates

### HFSAA
HFSAA states that it certifies restaurants, meat markets, dining halls, products, distributors and other food operations and lists a Bay Area chapter.

- https://www.hfsaa.org/
- https://www.hfsaa.org/chapters/

### IFANCA
IFANCA's published certification overview describes ingredient review, sanitation/process review, facility audit and contamination-risk review as central steps.

- https://ifanca.org/app/uploads/2025/09/HC_72_fin.pdf

The project should compare certifier standard, customer recognition, site feasibility, fees and supplier acceptance before choosing one.
