# DAMB GOOD LAH — HALAL Protein Pricing — 2026-09-07

## Executive conclusion

**HALAL protein cost is not automatically fatal to the current $11.95–$14.95 menu. Chicken may actually have substantial upside versus the prior planning assumptions.**

The biggest immediate discovery is a current public US Foods CHEF'STORE listing for **Koch raw certified-halal boneless skinless chicken thighs, 40 lb case, $85.49 = $2.14/lb**. A San Jose CHEF'STORE exists at 1731 W San Carlos St. This is potentially transformative versus Halal Foundry's current **$4.59/lb** delivered wholesale price.

However, the CHEF'STORE/Koch lane is **not approved yet**. Koch confirms the exact foodservice thigh product is halal certified, but the publicly retrieved sources do not establish that HFSAA accepts the certifier/slaughter standard for this SKU. HFSAA explicitly says that distributor/process approval applies only to products it approves and that products merely advertised as halal may not meet HFSAA standards. Therefore this price is a **high-priority candidate**, not yet a locked source.

The correct purchasing architecture is:

1. **Qualify the $2.14/lb Koch/CHEF'STORE thigh immediately.**
2. Keep **Halal Foundry at $4.59/lb delivered** as the clean HFSAA-certified reference/backup.
3. Obtain local wholesale quotes from **Modesto Food, Saba, Ar-Raheem and Al Salam**.
4. Use **Costco Business Center** as a cash-and-carry/delivery benchmark and potential emergency source if exact certification is accepted.
5. Compare beef on **finished usable cooked cost**, not raw sticker price.

# 7. Price bands I recommend using in negotiations

These are **commercial decision bands**, not religious or quality judgments.

## Boneless skinless halal chicken thigh

- **<= $2.50/lb landed:** excellent; lock/qualify aggressively.
- **$2.50–$3.00:** very strong.
- **$3.00–$3.75:** workable.
- **$3.75–$4.50:** margin pressure; keep looking.
- **>= $4.50:** use mainly as high-integrity backup unless quality/yield/service advantage justifies it.

The CHEF'STORE candidate changes our negotiating anchor: **we should not walk into local wholesale discussions assuming $4.59 is a good restaurant price.**

## Braising beef

- **<= $5.50/lb landed:** excellent if yield/texture pass.
- **$5.50–$6.25:** strong.
- **$6.25–$7.00:** workable.
- **$7.00–$7.75:** pressure; needs strong yield/quality.
- **> $7.75:** difficult for routine Padang Beef Rice at $14.95 unless portion/yield economics outperform.

Again, finished usable cost is what matters.

# 8. Immediate quote script — use identical request for apples-to-apples comparison

> We are launching DAMB GOOD LAH, a fully halal Malaysian catering and office-lunch operation in San Jose. We are qualifying recurring restaurant-volume protein suppliers. Please quote the following delivered to 5475 Snell Ave, San Jose, CA 95123:
>
> 1. boneless skinless halal chicken thighs — foodservice case, ideally ~40 lb;
> 2. beef shoulder clod, chuck roll, or your most economical halal boneless braising cut;
> 3. case weight and pack configuration;
> 4. $/case and $/lb;
> 5. delivery/fuel/freight charge;
> 6. minimum order;
> 7. delivery days and order cutoff;
> 8. recurring restaurant-volume pricing at approximately 1, 3, 5 and 10 cases/week as volume grows;
> 9. exact current halal certifier/certificate tied to these products;
> 10. whether products are hand slaughtered, mechanically slaughtered, stunned/non-stunned, and the source slaughter/processing facility if available.
>
> We are comparing **landed cost and certification**, so please include all mandatory fees rather than only base case pricing.

Do not reveal competitor pricing in the first request. Get their independent number first.

---

# 9. Quote comparison worksheet fields

For every response calculate:

`landed raw $/lb = (case price + freight + fuel + mandatory order fees) / case pounds`

Then after test cook:

`landed usable cooked $/lb = total landed case cost / usable cooked pounds`

Then:

`protein cost/meal = landed usable cooked $/g × commercial cooked portion grams`

This is the number that goes into the launch economics model.

# 10. Current recommendation

### Chicken
**Do not lock Halal Foundry as the primary yet.** It is a strong HFSAA-safe fallback, but the $2.14/lb CHEF'STORE/Koch candidate is too economically important not to investigate immediately.

The next factual question is binary:

> **Will HFSAA accept the exact Koch halal thigh SKU/certificate used by CHEF'STORE?**

If yes and San Jose stock is reliable, chicken economics improve dramatically.

If no, use the Koch/Restaurant Depot/Costco prices as evidence that commercial halal poultry can exist in the $2–$3/lb range and push HFSAA-compatible wholesalers for a better quote.

### Beef
Run a competitive quote between:
1. Ar-Raheem;
2. Halal Foundry;
3. Modesto Food if certifier acceptable;
4. Saba if exact HFSAA wholesale path is available;
5. Costco HFSAA/accepted-product benchmark only if certificate fits.

Target **$5.50–$6.25/lb landed** with a braise yield that produces the desired texture.

### Strategic conclusion
The pricing research does **not** currently argue for raising menu prices. It argues for **better procurement**.

Protein sourcing could plausibly save more per meal than many of the small operational cuts we were considering. Qualifying the right chicken source is now one of the highest-return launch tasks.
