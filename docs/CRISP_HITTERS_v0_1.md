# DAMB GOOD LAH — Crisp Hitters v0.1

**Status:** R0 development architecture. These are prototype starting points, not launch-validated recipes.

## Objective

Build five memorable crunchy components that make each launch dish feel intentional while improving trim utilization, prep efficiency and perceived value.

The crisp family must **not** become five versions of the same fried garnish. Each hitter needs its own texture, aroma and flavor job.

Canonical launch assignments:

1. **Padang Chicken Rice — chicken-skin glass**
2. **Padang Beef Rice — beef-fat shallot feather**
3. **Nasi Lemak Lah — ikan bilis–peanut crunch**
4. **Damb Good Mee Goreng — potato crackle**
5. **Padang Dry Curry Mee — curry tofu crag**
6. **CKT — no separate crisp hitter; visible halal chicken lap cheong + wok char are the protected textural/aromatic signatures.**

---

# 1. Governing rules

## 1.1 Every hitter must earn its place

A hitter graduates only if it materially improves at least two of:
- flavor identity;
- texture contrast;
- perceived value;
- trim/waste utilization;
- prep efficiency;
- inventory efficiency;
- delivery survival.

## 1.2 Shared technique is allowed; shared flavor is not

Common systems may include:
- drying racks;
- sheet-pan dehydration;
- fryer oil management;
- mandoline slicing;
- cooling racks;
- airtight dry storage after full cooling;
- measured seasoning after frying.

But the five hitters must remain sensorially distinct.

## 1.3 Secondary outputs are ingredients

If a crisp process generates useful fat/oil, capture it separately and cost it separately.

Examples:
- chicken skin → **rendered chicken fat**;
- beef trim + shallots → **beef/shallot fat**;
- shallot frying → **shallot-infused fat/oil**;
- ikan bilis frying oil → retain only if sensory testing proves a useful clean seafood note.

Do not assign economic value to secondary outputs until actual yield and use are measured.

## 1.4 Service-time fryer demand stays low

The preferred architecture is:
- produce crisp hitters before service;
- cool fully;
- portion quickly during assembly;
- avoid frying garnishes to order.

Any hitter that requires live service frying needs an exceptional sensory justification.

## 1.5 Delivery is part of the recipe

Each hitter must be tested:
- on-food at T0 / T15 / T30;
- separately packed at T0 / T15 / T30 only if the on-food version fails.

Do **not** add a separate garnish cup by default. Packaging and assembly cost must be justified by a meaningful quality gain.

# 2. Hitter A — Chicken-Skin Glass

## Dish
**Padang Chicken Rice**

## Sensory target
- thin;
- flat or lightly rippled;
- glassy/brittle rather than chewy;
- deep roasted chicken aroma;
- salty but not snack-seasoned;
- clean enough that the Padang curry remains the dominant flavor.

## Strategic value
This is potentially the highest-value hitter because one input can produce **two outputs**:
1. chicken-skin crisp;
2. rendered chicken fat for separate R&D, especially the chicken-fat CKT experiment.

## Procurement constraint
Current canonical thigh pricing work centers on **boneless skinless** thighs. Therefore chicken skin cannot be assumed to be free trim.

Test three sourcing paths:
- skin-on thigh differential;
- separate halal chicken-skin case/trim source;
- butcher/processor trim agreement.

The crisp only becomes a commercial win if landed skin cost + prep labor + yield are acceptable.

## Prototype method A — pressed oven glass

Starting batch:
- 1,000 g cleaned halal chicken skin
- 8–10 g fine salt as starting range

Process:
1. Remove obvious meat/connective tissue and scrape excessive attached fat where practical.
2. Weigh cleaned skin.
3. Dry aggressively.
4. Lay flat between parchment/silicone-lined sheet pans; use a second tray or rack to keep skin relatively flat while allowing fat to render.
5. Bake at moderate heat until most fat has rendered and skin structure is set.
6. Remove upper weight/tray and finish hotter only as needed for even deep-gold crispness.
7. Drain/cool on a rack with full airflow.
8. Capture rendered fat separately; weigh it.
9. Break/cut into final shards only after cooling if that gives better yield.

**R&D note:** exact oven temperatures/times are intentionally not locked at R0; use color, rendered-fat loss and final moisture/crispness as primary endpoints. A practical first test range is approximately 160–180°C render stage followed by a brief 185–200°C finish.

## Prototype method B — unpressed render

Run against Method A:
- skin laid flat on rack/sheet;
- moderate rendering phase;
- hotter finishing phase;
- no upper tray.

Potential advantage: easier evaporation and more blistering.

Potential disadvantage: curl, inconsistent piece size, harder assembly portioning.

## Starting portion
**4 g finished crisp / Chicken Rice** remains the current canonical starting point.

Test 3 g / 4 g / 5 g for perceived value.

## Record
- raw skin grams;
- cleaned skin grams;
- crisp grams;
- rendered chicken-fat grams;
- crisp yield %;
- fat yield %;
- active minutes;
- sheet-pan/oven occupancy;
- cost per 4 g portion;
- T0/T15/T30 crispness;
- grease migration onto curry/rice.

## Failure modes
- rubbery center → inadequate fat render / piece too thick;
- bitter dark edges → excessive final heat;
- curled pieces → insufficient flattening;
- greasy rather than brittle → inadequate draining/rendering;
- soggy in delivery → direct food contact too wet; test placement or dry-side cup only if needed.

# 3. Hitter B — Beef-Fat Shallot Feather

## Dish
**Padang Beef Rice**

## Sensory target
- feather-light crispy shallot;
- beefy aroma underneath, not waxy tallow heaviness;
- sweet-allium top note;
- shattering texture;
- no burnt bitterness.

## Strategic value
Use braising-beef trim to create the garnish system:

**beef trim → rendered beef fat → shallot crisp → beef/shallot cooking fat**

This turns trim into both texture and flavor instead of treating trim as simple loss.

## Prototype fat tests

Run the same shallot batch in:
- **B1:** 100% rendered beef fat;
- **B2:** 50% beef fat / 50% neutral canola;
- **B3:** neutral canola control.

The winner is determined by flavor + crispness + cost, not by ideology. Pure tallow only wins if it tastes materially better and does not coat the palate.

## Starting process

Starting batch:
- 500 g peeled shallots
- enough test fat/oil to allow free movement and even frying
- salt after frying

Process:
1. Slice uniformly with mandoline; first test approximately 1.5–2 mm.
2. Start shallots in cool/cold fat and raise heat gradually, stirring frequently.
3. Pull at **pale golden**, not deep brown; carryover continues after draining.
4. Drain immediately and spread in a single layer.
5. Season while warm.
6. Cool completely before storage.
7. Strain and capture the infused beef/shallot fat separately.

This cold-oil / pale-gold endpoint is consistent with established fried-shallot technique and helps reduce edge burning.

## Starting portion
**8 g / Beef Rice** remains the current canonical starting point.

Test 6 g / 8 g / 10 g.

## Optional flavor branch
Only after base texture passes:
- very light toasted coriander/white-pepper dust;
- no chile or curry powder by default because the Padang curry already owns the spice lane.

## Record
- raw beef trim available per case;
- rendered-fat yield;
- shallot raw → crisp yield;
- fat uptake/loss;
- grams crisp per batch;
- cost per 8 g portion;
- labor;
- T4/T8/T24 bench crispness target tests;
- T0/T15/T30 on-food test.

## Failure modes
- greasy/waxy palate → reduce beef-fat fraction;
- bitter → pulled too dark;
- limp → slices too thick / insufficient dehydration;
- garnish disappears into curry → adjust placement or portion, not automatically a side cup.

# 4. Hitter C — Ikan Bilis–Peanut Crunch

## Dish
**Nasi Lemak Lah**

## Sensory target
- unmistakably nasi lemak;
- crunchy anchovy + roasted peanut contrast;
- salty/nutty rather than sugary snack mix;
- enough volume to make the $12.95 dish feel abundant;
- remains separate in identity from the wet house sambal.

Traditional nasi lemak references consistently treat crisp fried ikan bilis and roasted peanuts as core accompaniments, which supports keeping this hitter close to the classic form rather than overengineering it.

## Core architecture
Prepare separately, combine only after both are fully cool:
- crisp ikan bilis;
- roasted/fried peanuts.

### Starting ratio tests by finished weight
- **C1:** 50% ikan bilis / 50% peanut
- **C2:** 40% ikan bilis / 60% peanut
- **C3:** 60% ikan bilis / 40% peanut

The canonical serving is currently **20–25 g total**.

## Ikan bilis process tests

Compare:
- **washed/rinsed + thoroughly dried**;
- **unrinsed control**.

Score:
- salt level;
- bitterness;
- clean fish aroma;
- crispness;
- oil life.

Fry until crisp and drain aggressively. Rasa Malaysia's nasi lemak method likewise fries the ikan bilis separately until crisp and pairs it with roasted peanuts.

## Peanut process tests

Compare:
- dry roasted;
- oil roasted/fried.

Dry roast should win if flavor is equal because it avoids unnecessary oil uptake and fryer contention.

## Optional branch after base passes
Test only one embellishment at a time:
- 5–10% neutral fried shallot;
- tiny chile-salt dust;
- no wet sambal coating for launch hitter because wet coating defeats the crispness job.

## Starting portion
Test **20 g / 22.5 g / 25 g**.

## Record
- ikan bilis raw cost / finished yield;
- peanut cost / finished yield;
- oil use;
- finished blend cost / 22.5 g portion;
- allergen-control burden;
- T0/T15/T30 crunch;
- salt perception when eaten with sambal + coconut rice.

## Failure modes
- too salty → rinse/dry or shift ratio toward peanut;
- too fishy → supplier/spec issue before recipe issue;
- peanut dominates → move ratio back toward ikan bilis;
- soggy → keep physically away from wet sambal during packing or use compartment geometry before adding another package.

# 5. Hitter D — Potato Crackle

## Dish
**Damb Good Mee Goreng**

## Sensory target
- craggy potato crunch;
- salty, lightly spiced;
- unmistakably potato;
- provides contrast against glossy chewy yellow noodles;
- should feel like a deliberate snack-like top note, not fries thrown on noodles.

## Strategic hypothesis
**The best commercial version may be made from potato peel/trim**, allowing the same potatoes to create:
- tender potato body for Mee Goreng;
- crisp potato garnish from what would otherwise be trim.

This is a hypothesis to prove, not a locked recipe.

## Prototype D1 — peel crackle

Process:
1. Scrub potatoes thoroughly **before** peeling.
2. Create controlled peel ribbons rather than paper-thin scraps.
3. Rinse excess surface starch if needed.
4. Dry extremely well.
5. First fry/dehydrate at moderate temperature.
6. Finish hotter for brittle texture if single-fry does not survive delivery.
7. Drain and season immediately.

Starting seasoning:
- salt;
- optional tiny white-pepper/chile component.

Avoid introducing a new complex seasoning blend until the potato itself works.

## Prototype D2 — broken potato crisp

Use the same par-cooked potato batch as Mee Goreng:
- reserve controlled amount;
- roughen/break into small craggy pieces;
- dry/chill as needed;
- fry until crisp.

This uses more potato but may produce a superior substantial crunch.

## Test decision
D1 wins if:
- peel flavor is clean;
- crispness is strong;
- prep labor is low;
- yield from normal peel stream supplies enough garnish.

D2 wins if it is substantially more delicious or delivery-stable.

## Starting portion
Test **8 g / 10 g / 12 g** finished crisp.

## Record
- potato raw weight;
- edible body yield;
- peel/trim weight;
- peel crisp yield;
- additional oil uptake;
- active minutes;
- cost per 10 g portion;
- 0/15/30-minute crunch;
- customer recognition: does it read as intentional potato crunch?

## Failure modes
- peel bitterness/earthiness → reject peel route;
- too thin/shattery → increase peel thickness or use D2;
- greasy → improve dehydration or fryer temp control;
- limp at T15 → double-fry or direct-on-food placement failure.

# 6. Hitter E — Curry Tofu Crag

## Dish
**Padang Dry Curry Mee**

## Sensory target
- irregular craggy pieces;
- airy/shattering exterior;
- light tofu center or fully crisp small bits depending test winner;
- warm curry aroma;
- survives contact with a relatively dry curry-coated noodle better than a soft tofu cube would.

## Strategic value
Use the same firm tofu SKU as Mee Goreng.

Preferred cascade:

**firm tofu block → clean cubes for Mee Goreng + trim/off-cuts for Curry Tofu Crag**

If trim volume is insufficient, deliberately allocate part of the same block; do not create a second tofu SKU solely for the garnish.

## Prototype coating tests

Serious Eats' comparative testing of fried tofu found that starch coating materially improves crispness; rice flour produced a delicate but relatively lasting crust while cornstarch produced a durable crisp crust. Use that as the basis for our own commercial tests.

Run:
- **E1:** 100% rice flour;
- **E2:** 70% rice flour / 30% cornstarch;
- **E3:** 50% rice flour / 50% cornstarch;
- **E4:** uncoated control.

## Process
1. Press/drain firm tofu to controlled moisture level.
2. Cut/tear trim into irregular 8–15 mm pieces.
3. Surface-dry.
4. Toss lightly in measured starch blend.
5. Fry until deeply crisp.
6. Drain thoroughly.
7. Apply curry seasoning **after frying** to reduce spice scorching.

## Curry dust prototype
Keep first version simple and tied to existing Padang pantry:
- ground coriander;
- cumin;
- turmeric;
- white pepper;
- salt;
- optional very small chile component.

Do not use a generic commercial curry powder until its flavor and HALAL status earn the SKU.

## Starting portion
Test **10 g / 12 g / 15 g**.

## Record
- tofu block weight;
- Mee Goreng cube yield;
- trim grams;
- crisp yield;
- starch grams / batch;
- oil use;
- cost per 12 g portion;
- T0/T15/T30 crispness;
- seasoning adhesion;
- curry-noodle interaction.

## Failure modes
- leathery → tofu too wet or pieces too large;
- coating falls off → surface moisture/precoat issue;
- soft by T15 → alter starch mix, size or fry profile;
- tastes like generic curry snack → reduce dry seasoning and let Padang sauce define lane.

# 7. Crisp hitter differentiation matrix

| Hitter | Texture | Main aroma | Flavor role | Secondary output | Target dish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken-skin glass | brittle / glassy | roasted chicken | savory clean crunch | chicken fat | Chicken Rice |
| Beef-fat shallot feather | airy / feathery | shallot + beef | aromatic sweet-savory | beef/shallot fat | Beef Rice |
| Ikan bilis–peanut crunch | chunky / hard-crisp | anchovy + roasted nut | salty/nutty classic nasi lemak | possible tested fish oil only | Nasi Lemak |
| Potato crackle | craggy / snack crisp | potato | starchy salty contrast | uses peel/trim | Mee Goreng |
| Curry tofu crag | porous / shattering | warm spice + tofu | spiced dry crunch | uses tofu trim | Curry Mee |

If two hitters begin tasting/texturing alike, one should be redesigned.

# 8. Shared crisp production choreography

Potential prep sequence to test:

1. **Chicken skin into oven first** — long passive render; capture fat.
2. **Beef trim rendering** can occur in parallel on another burner/oven zone.
3. Slice shallots during rendering.
4. Dry/press tofu while fats render.
5. Scrub/peel/par-cook potatoes; reserve peel stream.
6. Roast peanuts without using fryer if sensory result passes.
7. Fry ikan bilis.
8. Fry shallots in measured beef-fat test blend.
9. Fry potato crackle.
10. Fry tofu crag last among neutral items if oil-management plan permits and allergen/HALAL controls remain clean.

The exact fryer order must be finalized with allergen, cross-contact, HFSAA and oil-reuse requirements. Do not assume one shared oil is permissible until certification controls are resolved.

# 9. Hitter scorecard

Score 1–5:
- first-bite impact;
- dish identity;
- crunch intensity;
- T15 survival;
- T30 survival;
- grease perception;
- labor burden;
- equipment burden;
- trim utilization;
- cost / portion;
- portion speed;
- storage footprint.

## Kill rule
Kill or redesign a hitter if:
- it adds >~30 seconds of service assembly;
- it requires live frying during normal service without a major sensory benefit;
- it repeatedly fails T15 and a separate cup is not worth the added packaging/labor;
- its trim-utilization story is economically fake once labor is counted;
- it muddies the dish's protected flavor lane.

# 10. First crisp R&D session

Do **not** test all variables at once.

### Round 1 — texture mechanics
- chicken skin: pressed vs unpressed;
- shallot: 100% beef fat vs 50/50 vs neutral;
- ikan bilis: rinse/dry vs direct fry;
- potato: peel vs broken-parcook;
- tofu: rice flour vs rice/cornstarch blends.

No elaborate seasoning changes.

### Round 2 — portion + dish integration
Use winning mechanics and test:
- 3/4/5 g chicken skin;
- 6/8/10 g shallot;
- 20/22.5/25 g ikan bilis-peanut;
- 8/10/12 g potato;
- 10/12/15 g tofu.

### Round 3 — delivery
T0/T15/T30 direct-on-food.

Only add side-cup packaging for hitters that fail direct integration.

### Round 4 — scale
Produce enough hitter inventory for a 20-meal simulation and measure:
- total active labor;
- oven/fryer occupancy;
- yield;
- portion speed;
- breakage/waste;
- post-run leftovers.

# 11. Research basis

External technique references used to shape R0 experiments, not to substitute for our own validation:

- Serious Eats testing of starch-coated fried tofu: rice flour and cornstarch materially improve crispness and differ in durability.
- Serious Eats fried-shallot method: uniform thin slicing, gradual frying, pull at pale gold because carryover continues, drain thoroughly.
- Rasa Malaysia nasi lemak: ikan bilis fried crisp and served with roasted peanuts as classic accompaniments.
- General chicken-skin technique literature consistently emphasizes aggressive drying and adequate fat rendering before final crisping.

Commercial graduation still requires DAMB GOOD LAH's own measured R1–R5 validation.
