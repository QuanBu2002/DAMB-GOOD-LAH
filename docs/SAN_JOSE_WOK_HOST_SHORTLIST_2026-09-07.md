# DAMB GOOD LAH — San Jose Wok-Host Shortlist — 2026-09-07

## Objective

Identify San Jose / near-San-Jose restaurants with a high probability of native wok capability plus public service hours that create a meaningful off-hour production window for DAMB GOOD LAH.

Public dining hours do not prove kitchen-idle hours. Actual prep arrival, receiving, catering, cleaning, landlord/permit constraints and owner willingness must be confirmed directly.

## Canonical scheduling priority

Prioritize **Monday-Friday production access**. A host does **not** need to provide all five weekdays to be attractive; **four reliable weekdays is fully acceptable**, and even 2-3 weekdays can still serve as a useful validation host.

Ranking rule:
1. four or five usable weekdays = strongest schedule fit;
2. three weekdays = strong pilot fit;
3. one or two weekdays = preserve on list as supplemental / pilot / redundancy;
4. weekend-only windows = keep for R&D, catering and contingency, but rank below weekday hosts.

Do not delete otherwise strong candidates simply because they cannot offer every weekday. The goal is to assemble reliable M-F production coverage, potentially using one primary host plus a secondary host if needed.

## Pilot strategy

Primary goal is **reliable Monday-Friday coverage**, but a single host does not need to cover all five days.

Preferred sequence:
1. First seek a **4-5 weekday host** with acceptable hot-line/wok capability: Elements, House of Genji, Yuki Willow Glen, then retrofit-capable dinner-only kitchens.
2. In parallel pursue **Tee Nee Thai** for Mon/Tue/Wed because native wok probability is high.
3. Preserve **China Inn Monday** and **Thai Orchid Mon/Fri** as supplemental native-wok lanes, pilot days and redundancy.
4. If no single host gives four weekdays, combine complementary hosts only if the added storage/logistics burden remains lower than a commissary.

The first objective is to prove that an off-hour host arrangement can deliver:
- real wok heat and recovery;
- 20-30 meal batch production;
- clean prep-to-pack flow;
- predictable reset time;
- storage compatibility;
- a fixed monthly cost materially below equivalent commissary economics.

## First-call questions

1. Do you have one or more commercial wok burners / wok wells? What make/model or BTU if known?
2. What time does kitchen staff actually arrive on the target weekdays?
3. Is the kitchen used for catering or prep while the dining room is closed?
4. Which Monday-Friday daytime blocks could be guaranteed every week?
5. Would ownership consider a recurring off-hour kitchen-use agreement for a separately permitted and insured food business?
6. Would ownership execute Santa Clara County rental-kitchen paperwork if required?
7. Can DAMB GOOD LAH have dedicated labeled fridge/freezer/dry storage?
8. Can we use or maintain dedicated woks, pans, utensils and cutting boards?
9. Is a fryer available? Can oil be changed/reset as needed?
10. How much prep-table and packing space is available?
11. What fixed monthly payment would make the arrangement worthwhile?

## Negotiation framework

Do not lead with hourly rent. Sell dead-hour monetization:
- fixed recurring weekday block;
- fixed monthly payment;
- no dining-room use required;
- our own labor/food/insurance;
- documented sanitation/reset;
- leave before their operating crew needs the line.

Initial economic target remains roughly **$750-$1,500/month** depending on number of weekdays, storage and equipment access, with a preference for a 60-day pilot before a larger commitment.
