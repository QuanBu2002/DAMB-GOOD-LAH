# DAMB GOOD LAH — Launch Execution Masterplan — 2026-09-07

## Objective

Build DAMB GOOD LAH into a repeatable, authentic, fully HALAL Malaysian food operation that can sell aggressively priced food **without depending on high marketplace commissions or premature fixed cost**.

The launch is validated only when the business can repeatedly:

1. produce food that passes the culinary standard;
2. produce it safely and within the chosen HALAL control system;
3. hit economic throughput targets;
4. survive a realistic delivery window;
5. generate positive contribution on actual paid orders; and
6. obtain repeat demand, especially from office/corporate accounts.

## Governing rule

> **Prove demand first. Add fixed cost second.**

Do not optimize the business for theoretical scale before the first operating loop works.

---

# 1. Critical path

The true critical path is:

**HALAL/certifier feasibility → kitchen/process/wok clearance → supplier/SKU clearance → test-kitchen recipe/process validation → 20/30/50-meal production validation → paid office pilots → repeat ordering → scale.**

Everything else may be built in parallel, but none of it can substitute for these gates.

## Blockers that can stop launch entirely

1. No certifier-acceptable operating arrangement.
2. No kitchen that permits our real process.
3. No CKT-quality heat solution.
4. No compliant halal chicken/beef chain of custody.
5. No commercially viable halal chicken lap cheong.
6. Processed ingredient SKU that fails certification with no acceptable substitute.
7. Food safety / County permit incompatibility.
8. Production throughput materially below economic threshold.
9. Delivery degradation severe enough that the food no longer represents the brand.
10. Paid demand does not repeat.

# 2. Launch phases

## Phase 0 — architecture and preflight

**Goal:** eliminate avoidable dead ends before paying meaningful fixed cost.

Complete:
- HALAL certifier preflight.
- Snell / iKitchens / California Cookery scorecard.
- County operator permit/process preflight.
- halal meat quotes + documentation.
- halal lap-cheong sourcing path.
- processed-SKU review list.
- packaging short list.
- direct-ordering/payment architecture.
- delivery fulfillment options.
- test-kitchen measurement system.

**Exit gate:** at least one kitchen has a credible path through HALAL + process + CKT + equipment + economics.

## Phase 1 — culinary + operating validation

**Goal:** turn the development recipes into measurable commercial procedures.

Run controlled sessions for:
- Padang curry.
- chicken preparation.
- beef braise.
- coconut rice.
- house sambal.
- mee goreng sauce.
- CKT seasoning/chile lane.
- tofu, potato and crispy garnish prep.
- roti + dip.

Measure:
- raw weight.
- usable cooked yield.
- prep time.
- active labor time.
- holding/reheat behavior.
- waste.
- final portion weight.
- 0/15/30-minute quality.

**Exit gate:** each launch dish has a repeatable recipe/process, measured yield and acceptable delivery quality.

## Phase 2 — production simulations

### Simulation A — 10 meals
Purpose: station mechanics and packaging choreography.

### Simulation B — 20 meals
Purpose: minimum office-drop architecture.

### Simulation C — second independent 20-meal run
Purpose: test reproducibility rather than one lucky session.

### Simulation D — 30 meals
Purpose: identify batching and equipment bottlenecks.

### Simulation E — 50 meals
Purpose: determine whether the current facility/team can scale without quality collapse.

For every simulation measure:
- total paid person-hours.
- prep vs service vs cleanup hours.
- main-equivalents produced.
- main-equivalents / paid kitchen hour.
- order errors.
- rework/remakes.
- food waste.
- packaging time.
- temperature at pack, handoff and 30 minutes.
- quality at 0/15/30 minutes.
- bottleneck station.
- equipment waiting time.
- actual kitchen cost allocated.

**Target:** consumer >=18 main-equivalents / total paid kitchen hour; constrained corporate >=24/hour.

## Phase 3 — controlled paid pilots

Start with deliberately small customer set.

Preferred sequence:
1. known internal/friendly paid group where feedback quality is high;
2. 1–2 office/lab/clinic groups;
3. repeat order from the same group;
4. second independent recurring account;
5. then wider outbound.

A first order proves very little. A repeat order is much stronger evidence of product-market fit.

## Phase 4 — controlled launch

Only after Phase 3:
- launch direct ordering broadly.
- use marketplace primarily for discovery/overflow.
- expand corporate prospecting.
- add kitchen hours only when utilized.
- add labor only when bottleneck data demands it.

## Phase 5 — scale decision

Consider:
- 40-hour / unlimited kitchen tier.
- second production block.
- additional employee.
- route clustering.
- wider delivery radius.
- catering marketplaces.
- dedicated facility.

Only after measured demand and contribution justify them.

# 3. Workstreams

## A. HALAL certification

Primary: **HFSAA**.

Warm backup: **American Halal Foundation (AHF)** for a second assessment if HFSAA rejects the shared-kitchen architecture or imposes commercially impossible controls.

Comparison/third opinion: **IFANCA**, subject to confirming that its scope fits a restaurant/catering operator.

Do not certifier-shop merely to find the weakest standard. Redundancy is for feasibility and operational fit, not lowering integrity.

Required certifier outputs before lease:
- written facility feasibility.
- shared equipment rule.
- fryer/oil rule.
- storage rule.
- sanitation/reset rule.
- utensils/smallwares rule.
- supplier documentation requirements.
- processed-SKU review process.
- employee training requirements.
- audit cadence.
- startup + annual cost.
- public claim/logo rules.

## B. Kitchen

Primary: **The Commissary — 5475 Snell Ave**.

Warm backup A: **iKitchens**.
- public pricing starts around $450–$500/month depending package/page.
- 24-hour access.
- ability to bring equipment / custom-build configurations is publicly advertised.
- $1,000 annual licensing fee is currently stated in FAQ.

Warm backup B: **California Cookery — 738 Chestnut St**.
- 24/7 operation advertised.

Emergency bridge:
- another already-permitted commercial kitchen may be used only if it passes the same HALAL/process/equipment gates.

## C. Proteins

Beef primary candidates:

Emergency retail bridge:

## D. Halal chicken lap cheong

Primary sourcing path: **Bestt Impex**.

Backup: **CKK Paradiso**.

- do **not** substitute generic sausage.
- do **not** redesign the dish around supply failure.

## G. Packaging

Packaging is a quality system, not just a cost line.

Primary quote candidates:
- Restaurant Supply Hub — Sunnyvale.
- To-Go Drop2Drop — San Jose.
- Dao Distribution — can consolidate packaging with pantry orders.

Warm backups:
- Safra Distribution — Fremont/Bay Area delivery.
- Plaztigo.
- Central Cash & Carry / Restaurant Depot for emergency stock.

Two packaging systems should be validated:
1. consumer/direct delivery;
2. corporate/boxed lunch.

Do not custom-print early. Use labels/stickers until volumes justify custom packaging minimums and the container format is stable.

## H. Ordering + payment

Recommended launch primary: **Square Free / direct online ordering** unless test results show a specific operational need for a more expensive restaurant stack.

Why:
- no fixed software fee at Free tier.
- direct online ordering.
- current online payment fee is higher than in-person but far below 25–30% marketplace commissions.
- can upgrade later without redesigning the business around a costly POS.

Warm backup:
- DoorDash Online Ordering / Commerce Platform as a commission-free direct ordering channel subject to payment/contract review.

Do not buy Toast hardware/software before there is an operational reason to do so.

## I. Delivery

Corporate preferred:
- one coordinated drop to one handoff person.
- price delivery as a route/drop cost, not one courier per meal.

Direct consumer options:
1. pickup.
2. merchant-arranged local delivery.
3. Uber Direct / DoorDash Drive type flat-fee white-label courier.
4. marketplace as discovery/overflow.

Uber Direct publicly advertises 0% commission and per-delivery pricing; actual local quotes must be measured.

No delivery platform is accepted based on advertised minimum pricing alone. Measure:
- actual fee.
- driver wait.
- lateness.
- food temperature.
- loss/damage.
- support failure rate.

## J. Sales

Primary engine: direct office/corporate acquisition.

Channels:
- office managers.

Redundancy channels:

# 4. Failure triggers

## Switch kitchen if
- certifier rejects architecture;
- wok test fails and compliant equipment cannot be added;
- raw meat/cooling process is not allowed;
- weekday equipment cannot be reliably reserved;
- mandatory fees materially invalidate pilot economics.

## Switch supplier if
- certification lapses or becomes unacceptable;
- two consecutive fill failures affect production;
- delivered quality fails specification;
- landed cost materially exceeds backup after yield adjustment;
- lead time is incompatible with working inventory.

## Change packaging if
- leakage >1% of realistic delivery tests;
- condensation materially damages fried/crisp components;
- CKT/noodles steam into unacceptable texture;
- packaging time creates a station bottleneck;
- total cost exceeds target without a demonstrated quality benefit.

## Change menu execution if
- a process repeatedly prevents throughput targets;
- but first redesign the **process**, not the culinary identity.

## Do not scale if
- contribution is negative at measured throughput;
- waste remains uncontrolled;
- food quality fails after delivery;
- repeat ordering is weak;
- operation depends on owner heroics rather than a reproducible station system.

---

# 6. Cash discipline

Before proven repeat demand, avoid:
- long kitchen commitments.
- custom packaging MOQs.
- unnecessary POS subscriptions.
- full-time labor commitments.
- large marketing retainers.
- marketplace-funded customer acquisition where the economics are unclear.
- dedicated facility buildout.

Spend early money only where it resolves a launch gate or produces measurable learning.
