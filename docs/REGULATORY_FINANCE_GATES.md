# Regulatory + Financial Gates Before Public Launch

## HALAL hard gates

- DAMB GOOD LAH is designed as a **fully HALAL** operation; no pork enters commercial inventory or production.
- Char kway teow requires **certified halal chicken lap cheong / la chang** that preserves the authentic Chinese-sausage role.
- Select a halal certifier and preflight the **exact facility + process** before signing a long-term kitchen commitment.
- Obtain acceptable documentation for halal chicken, beef and the lap cheong supplier.
- Review processed SKUs including belacan, fish sauce, soy/dark soy, kecap manis, frying oil, ghee and other seasonings with the chosen certifier.
- Confirm receiving, storage, labeling, dedicated/approved utensils, fryer/equipment use, sanitation and staff-training controls.
- Shared-kitchen non-halal exposure is a hard facility question; do not assume sanitation alone makes a site certifiable.
- Do not make a public certified-HALAL claim until certification/approval is complete.

## Food-facility + operating gates

- Confirm the exact operator/food-facility permit structure with Santa Clara County Environmental Health for the chosen shared kitchen and delivery/pickup/catering model.
- Do not assume the commissary landlord's permit automatically covers DAMB GOOD LAH.
- Confirm required food-safety manager/handler certifications for ownership and staff roles.
- Confirm business registration, seller/sales-tax handling, insurance limits, workers' compensation/payroll obligations, and commissary-required documentation.
- Confirm approved storage, cooling, hot-holding/time-control, raw/RTE separation, allergen handling and pickup/driver handoff procedures.
- CKT facility gate: verify actual burner output/recovery and simultaneous equipment availability before treating a kitchen as suitable for the full launch menu.

## Financial gates

Before final menu-price lock, replace planning ranges with:

1. Measured cooked/usable yields.
2. Actual halal supplier landed cost, certification documents and pack size.
3. Final commercial portion weights.
4. Consumer and corporate packaging case cost / package map.
5. Paid prep/service/cleanup hours.
6. Consumer and corporate main-equivalents per **total paid kitchen hour**.
7. Real waste/remake/spoilage rate.
8. Actual direct/payment, courier and marketplace costs.
9. Sales mix, corporate order size, corporate menu concentration and roti attach rate.
10. Current kitchen/storage/insurance/permit/certification/accounting costs.
11. Employee incentive cost and verified productivity/waste benefit.
12. Marketing/customer-acquisition cost by direct, office-captain, paid social and marketplace channels.

## Current planning targets

- Consumer economic productivity: **>=18 mains / total paid kitchen hour**.
- Corporate constrained-menu productivity: **>=24 mains / total paid kitchen hour**.
- Prefer **20+ meals per office/corporate drop**.
- Consumer packaging base target: **~$1.15/main**.
- Corporate packaging base target: **~$0.65/meal**.
- Waste base target: **~5%**, with corporate preorders expected to do better.
- Do not use price increases as the first fix for weak economics; test throughput, channel density, labor staggering, procurement, packaging and waste first.

## Marketplace warning

At $11.95/$12.95/$14.95 consumer pricing, 25-30% marketplace commissions are not a viable economic foundation. Use direct ordering, direct pickup, corporate/catering and lower-commission marketplace discovery first; only pay higher commissions when measured incremental demand produces higher total contribution.
