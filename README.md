# DAMB GOOD LAH

**HALAL Malaysian-inspired, delivery-first food concept built around value, office drops, catering and direct ordering.**

This repository is the durable project archive for DAMB GOOD LAH: culinary development, menu architecture, test-kitchen records, operating design, HALAL controls, delivery testing, economics, regulatory work and launch planning.

## Canonical brand + HALAL direction

- Canonical name: **DAMB GOOD LAH**. Legacy `DANG GOOD LAH` / `DAMN GOOD LAH` naming is retired.
- The commercial operation is designed to be **fully HALAL**.
- **No pork enters commercial inventory or production.**
- Char kway teow preserves its Chinese-sausage identity with **certified halal chicken lap cheong / la chang**, not a generic chicken sausage.
- Use Malaysian Muslim precedent first; adapt only when the U.S. supply chain or certifier requires it.
- Do not make a public certified-HALAL claim until the chosen certifier approves the facility, ingredient SKUs and operating controls.

## Business model

The concept is **not** being designed around one high-commission delivery-app order at a time.

Primary economic lanes:
1. Office and corporate drop-offs
2. Catering / preorders
3. Direct pickup and direct ordering
4. Repeat direct customers
5. Low-commission marketplace discovery / overflow

Large office orders should preferably be 20+ meals and can be constrained to 2-3 main choices when appropriate to create real batching savings. Discounts are justified by operational density, not simply because an order is labeled catering.

## Operating principles already solved

- Simplify operations without flattening food identity.
- Preserve four distinct flavor lanes: Padang curry; house sambal with dish-specific finish; Mamak-style mee goreng sauce; protected CKT seasoning/chile/high-heat execution.
- One main chicken preparation serves multiple dishes with different final formats.
- Yellow noodles are shared where appropriate; CKT retains fresh flat rice noodles.
- Shared potato prep serves mee goreng body and crisp finish.
- One firm tofu SKU serves tender and crisp applications.
- Advance-prep dry hitters where safely validated to protect service-time fryer capacity.
- CKT remains a protected benchmark. If a facility cannot provide adequate burner heat/recovery, CKT does not launch from that facility.
- Delivery quality is part of recipe development: 0/15/30-minute texture, temperature, packaging and carry testing remain mandatory.

## Economic targets

Current planning targets:
- Consumer economic productivity: **>=18 sellable main-equivalents per total paid kitchen hour**
- Corporate/constrained-menu productivity: **>=24 per total paid kitchen hour**
- Food waste: **~5% base or lower after stabilization**
- Consumer packaging: **~$1.15 base or better if delivery quality permits**
- Corporate packaging: **~$0.65 base**
- Marketplace dependence: reduce over time; direct + corporate should dominate

At the current v0.2 planning model, the new value prices can work only if those operating improvements are real. Price increases are not the first response to inefficiency.

## Evidence discipline

Recipes, portions, prices and operational thresholds remain development assumptions until physically measured. Missing cost/yield data means **unknown**, not zero. Commercial decisions use finished usable yield, landed supplier cost, paid labor, kitchen time, packaging, waste, delivery/channel cost and actual sales mix.
